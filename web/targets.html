Print a capture mat

A calibrated length in shot is the single highest-value thing you can add to a capture. It satisfies scale-reference — the one dimension no amount of processing can recover afterwards — and it gives ground sample distance directly, needing no focal length, no sensor size and no distance measurement.

Print at 100% / "Actual size". Not "Fit to page".
Then measure the printed bar with a ruler and enter what you measured in the app. Printers scale silently: a real card printed from a 150 mm design came out at 137.5 mm, which would have made every measurement from that session 8% wrong — and nothing downstream could have detected it.

How to use it

  1. Print at 100%, on matte paper if you have it. Gloss reflects the light back at the camera and blows out the very patches you printed it for.
  2. Measure the black bar, end to end, with the best ruler you have. Write the number in the box on the mat — you only do this once per printed card.
  3. Lay it flat and put the object in the dashed square. A creased mat is no longer a straight line of known length.
  4. Enter the measured length in the app under What is in shot. Not the nominal one.
  5. Keep the mat in frame as you orbit. The markers differ on each side so some stay visible whatever angle you are at.

Already have a Plumb card?

Use it. Its calibrated width is a perfectly good scale reference here — select Plumb calibration card and enter your measured width. It carries no colour patches, so the colour dimension stays unmet and the class caps below reference; everything else works.

No card at all?

A steel rule or any ruler works, and is what most conservation labs already have. Lay it in the same plane as the widest part of the object, and enter the span you will point at. What matters is that a known length is in the photograph, not that it came from us.
